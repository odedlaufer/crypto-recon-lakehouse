Live market feeds lose data, and most pipelines never notice

Coinbase's own documentation warns that messages on its live trade channel can be dropped, and that consumers must detect the gaps and backfill them. Most pipelines simply store whatever arrives, so a dashboard built on them is quietly wrong.

This project proves the stored data is complete: it checks every minute against an independent source, finds every missing trade by its id, and repairs the data, keeping a record of both the before and the after.

How it works: capture, refine, reconcile, detect, repair

1

Capture

A Python producer streams every trade from Coinbase's WebSocket, validates it and lands batches in Databricks. A watchdog restarts capture if it ever stops.

2

Refine

A Lakeflow pipeline builds bronze, silver and gold tables: deduplicated trades, data-quality rules with a quarantine, and 1-minute candles.

3

Reconcile

dbt compares every minute with Coinbase's official candles, which must pass a Soda write-audit-publish gate first.

4

Detect

Trade ids are consecutive per product, so any jump is a provable gap, down to a single missing trade.

5

Repair

Each gap's exact ids are fetched from the REST API and loaded, and the affected minutes are reconciled again.

6

Observe

SLOs, anomaly detection and Slack alerts. This page and the dashboard update after every daily run.

Proven by breaking it on purpose

Every failure mode is injected for real on the live system, and must be caught by the right check.

CH-1

Drop 5% of trades

899 trades dropped at random over 20 minutes: 853 gaps.

All detected and repaired within a day. It also exposed a backfill limit, now fixed: nearby gaps are fetched together.

CH-2

Kill the producer

Capture stopped for 10 minutes: 17,740 trades never seen.

Detected and recovered the next morning, with no one involved.

CH-3

Poison the stream

Negative prices, bad sides, future timestamps, unknown products, broken JSON.

All 7 quarantined by the exact rule; none reached the tables.

CH-4

Corrupt the source of truth

An official candle with its high below its low.

Quality gate failed, publishing blocked, published data untouched.

And an end-to-end integration test

One recorded hour of real trades with three known gaps is replayed through the real pipeline in its own catalog. It must find exactly those gaps and reproduce the exact same result every run.

Reliability targets, last 7 days

Batch gate: a failed audit means a blocked publish, as designed. The CH-4 chaos test on 2026-11-23 counts here until it leaves the 7-day window.

Decisions and trade-offs

The constraint was a free platform with no always-on compute. Each decision is recorded as an ADR in the repository.

Capture runs outside Databricks

Constraint The free tier allows no outbound internet and no always-on compute.

Decision A containerised producer on GitHub Actions lands files in a Unity Catalog volume, with a watchdog for restarts.

Trade-off Scheduled runs can start late; the gap repair absorbs it.

Batch files to fit the quota

Problem Writing a file every 30 seconds exhausted the daily quota.

Decision 10-minute batches and one load per day.

Result About 5× fewer storage API calls; freshness is now measured at load.

Detect gaps by trade id, not by comparing candles

Options Compare candles only, or use trade-id continuity.

Decision Trade ids, after measuring that they are consecutive per product.

Result Finds gaps as small as one trade, including ones that stay within candle tolerances.

Don't trust the "source of truth" blindly

Finding Official candles sometimes miss trades the exchange itself reported.

Decision A separate status for official errors, counted honestly rather than hidden.

A contract-based quality gate

Options Soda Core or Great Expectations for the write-audit-publish gate.

Decision Soda data contracts on Spark; the licence (source-available, not OSI) is documented.

With a paid platform, I would

  • stream through Kafka or Kinesis instead of landing files
  • run the pipeline continuously for second-level freshness
  • host capture on a managed container service with health checks
  • split dev and prod workspaces with service principals

What building it revealed

The exchange's own candles are sometimes wrong

On about 2% of minutes, Coinbase's official candles miss trades that its own trade feed proves happened.

Small gaps hide inside tolerances

A two-trade gap moved a minute's volume by 0.001%, inside the match tolerance. Only trade-id continuity caught it.

One bad timestamp can drop good data

A trade stamped in the future would have pushed the deduplication watermark ahead and discarded real trades. Found while designing a chaos test, fixed before it happened.

Fresh environments expose hidden assumptions

The first integration-test run found that the quality gate relied on a table that only existed in development.
